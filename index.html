Custom data tools for small businesses that run on spreadsheets.

You already have the data. It is spread across your purchasing system, your email, three spreadsheets, and someone’s memory. Varinta builds a hard-coded tool that pulls it together and gives you a straight answer, on your own computer, in your own files.

The difference worth knowing

These tools are hard-coded. They are not an AI wrapper.

A lot of what is being sold right now is a thin layer over a language model. Every time it runs, it sends your data to a vendor and charges you for the privilege. The answer can also change between runs, because the model is guessing.

Varinta builds hard-coded, deterministic tools. The rules are written in code, not guessed by a model. The same inputs produce the same outputs, every time, and you can ask exactly why a number came out the way it did.

Built to be boring and to outlive the project

Your data never leaves your accounts

Tools read from your Google Drive, OneDrive, or computer and write results back to the same place. Varinta hosts nothing and stores nothing. How access works.

The output is a file you already know

Usually an Excel or CSV file sitting in a folder you own. If the tool stopped running tomorrow, every file you have is still there and still readable.

No Varinta subscription

You pay for the build. The hard-coded logic is yours to run with no per-seat or per-run fee from Varinta. If a tool needs an outside service account, it is opened in your name and you see the bill.

Scoped to one problem at a time

Small, finished projects with a written scope and an end date, not an open-ended engagement that quietly becomes a retainer.

Who this is for

Consumer-facing businesses under roughly $50 million in revenue — clinics, shops, service companies, distributors, multi-location operators. Usually the owner is still the person who knows where everything is, and that is exactly the bottleneck worth removing.

If you are already running a system that does this well, you do not need Varinta. The work worth paying for is the twenty percent your software will not do.
