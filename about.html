About

A note from the person who does the work

Varinta is one person. You talk to me from the first conversation through handoff, and nobody is handed your account.

I am a West Point graduate and a former U.S. Army logistics officer. Since the Army I have spent more than ten years in program management and operations, including serving as chief operating officer of a distribution branch.

Army logistics is parts, trucks, schedules, and people, with a deadline and incomplete information. Distribution is the same problem with invoices attached. Both come down to one question: what is true right now, and what do you do about it?

Running a distribution branch meant living inside the problem this business exists to fix. Purchasing data in one system, sales in another, the real numbers in a spreadsheet somebody maintains by hand, and a decision due Monday either way. Every tool I build started as a version of that.

So you will not spend the first two meetings explaining how your business works. Purchase orders, backorders, margin, cycle counts, vendor terms, month-end — I already use those words. I also know which fields in operational data are trustworthy and which ones everybody quietly ignores, which is the difference between a useful tool and a confident wrong answer.

The way I work comes from the same place. One scoped build at a time, with a written scope and a date it ends. Output in Excel and CSV files in your own folders, not locked inside an app you keep paying for. Rules that are hard-coded, so you can inspect them. The narrowest access that works, revocable by you at any time — how that works is written out on Security & Your Data. And if part of a build is fragile, you hear it during scoping, not after delivery.

Anyone hiring a small shop should ask who maintains this in two years. The honest answer has three parts. The tools are simple and the output is plain files, so if a tool stopped tomorrow your data is still yours and still opens in Excel. You can have the source code, so you are not locked to me. And I answer the phone; this is the business, not a side project.

I am in San Diego. Discovery is done in person when that helps, because walking the actual process beats a call. Most of a build does not need anyone in the room, so remote work is fine too.

If something here sounds like your situation, write to me and say what you are trying to find out.
