About

Built by someone who has run the back office, not just read about it

Varinta is a one-person shop in San Diego. That is deliberate — you talk to the person doing the work, from the first conversation through handoff.

Background

West Point graduate. Former U.S. Army logistics officer. Ten-plus years in program management and operations since, including serving as chief operating officer of a distribution branch.

Army logistics is not a theory job. It is parts, trucks, schedules, and people, with a hard deadline and incomplete information. Distribution is the same problem with invoices attached. Both come down to the same question: what is actually true right now, and what do you do about it?

Running a distribution branch meant living inside the exact mess this business exists to fix — purchasing data in one system, sales in another, the real numbers in a spreadsheet somebody maintains by hand, and a decision due Monday regardless. Every tool Varinta builds started as a version of that problem.

Why that background matters to you

I already speak your language

Purchase orders, backorders, margin, cycle counts, vendor terms, month-end. You will not spend the first two meetings explaining how your business works.

I know which data is lying

Operational data is always imperfect. Knowing which fields are trustworthy and which ones everybody quietly ignores is the difference between a useful tool and a confident wrong answer.

I scope like an operator

A project with no end date is a project that eats your attention. Scope, deliverable, out-of-scope list, end date — in writing, before the build starts.

How I work

  • Small, finished projects. One problem at a time, with a written scope and a date it ends.
  • Boring output on purpose. Excel and CSV files in your own folders, not data locked inside an app you would have to keep paying for.
  • Deterministic code. Hard-coded rules you can inspect, not a model guessing at your numbers with a meter running.
  • The narrowest access that works. One folder, read-only where read-only will do, revocable by you at any time. See Security & Your Data.
  • Straight answers about risk. If part of a build is fragile, you hear it during scoping, not after delivery.

The fair question: who maintains this in two years?

Anyone hiring a small shop should ask it. The honest answer has three parts. First, the tools are deliberately simple and the output is plain files — if the tool stopped tomorrow, your data is still yours and still readable in Excel. Second, you can have the source code, so you are not locked to me. Third, I am here and I answer the phone; the work is the business, not a side project.
